## Why no partial landing

The controlling precedent is the sibling manifest **NODE C.93**, whose clause (ii) is the same
kind of `#check` census. `leanfinal/Uniformity/ChapC/C93.lean:26-31` states the rule explicitly
in the landed file:

> *Clause (ii), the census `#check` suite* … is the `C93Census` block of
> `leanspec/Leanspec/ChapC.lean` and lands in `leanfinal` only when the named items themselves
> land — the census names are fleet-owned and NOT duplicated here.

C.93 could still land because it has an independent clause (i), the threshold arithmetic. C.106
has no such clause. Three options were considered and rejected:

1. **Land a three-row manifest** (`s2Witness`, `shear_onesided_iff`, `wconj_invariants`) with the
   other seven rows commented out. Rejected: the manifest's whole content is the assertion
   *"this, and exactly this, is what D and F consume from §10"*. A file that checks 3/10 rows and
   is named `C106.lean` reads downstream as a landed manifest and would let a D- or F-side
   consumer conclude that the §10 supply is in the tree. That is the weakening the
   BLOCKED-not-weakened rule exists to prevent.
2. **Re-declare the missing names locally.** Rejected for the same reason the C.38a clause-2
   record gives for `dvResPoly`: these are public node declarations, not private helpers, so a
   local copy forks the definition and puts two `gentow2_B`s in the tree. Worse here — five of
   the seven are `axiom` stubs, so a local copy would be introducing *new axioms* into
   `leanfinal` under a documentation node.
3. **Restate the manifest as prose only, with no `#check`s.** Rejected: prose that names ten
   declarations without elaborating them is exactly the thing a `#check` suite exists to
   prevent (a manifest whose rows are not machine-checked is a comment, and comments are
   unverified claims). It would also silently retire the signed signature.
