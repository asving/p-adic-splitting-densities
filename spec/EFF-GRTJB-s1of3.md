## 6. BOUNDARY SELF-AUDIT (prose genre, rule 9)

GRTJB carries **zero `\tag{` displays** in 3327 lines. Nothing announces "this span is one statement". What it has instead: one large Markdown matrix, eight bold-headed dated round blocks, ~176 strike markers, bold column headers inside a preamble, and five numbered cell notes. ### 6.0 The rule applied

> **A unit is the smallest span carrying one complete claim together with its proof obligations.**

Operationally, the same four-part test the HE7 pilot used, applied in this order:

1. **Does the span have a proof obligation of its own?** A displayed lemma, a derivation, a recount under a stated criterion, or a machine verdict — yes. A pointer sentence or a heading — no.
2. **Is the span cited BY DESIGNATION from elsewhere?** `⟨n1⟩`–`⟨n5⟩`, `(a1)`, `(a2)`, `(b)`, `(c)`, `(e)`, “summary point (1)”, “row 11 (a2)”, “surface 14” are all cited by name from other spans. A named, separately-cited object is addressable, therefore a unit.
3. **Does the span have its OWN supersession history?** In a note with eight repair rounds this is the dominant splitter.
4. **Does the span have its own conditionality or its own consumer set?**

Proofs are NOT units (rule 1 makes DERIVATION a field). **One genre-specific addition for this note: a REPAIR-ROUND DISPOSITION is a unit iff it moves a fence, a census, a definition, an evidence class or a grade; otherwise it merges into its round's assembled unit.** That criterion is stated at `.47` and `.54` and applied consistently.
