## 6. BOUNDARY SELF-AUDIT (prose-genre pilot)

HE7 carries **zero `\tag{` displays**. Nothing in it announces "this span is one statement". What it has instead: bold `LEMMA/THEOREM/DEFINITION/COROLLARY/CONVENTION/FINDING` openers inside blockquotes (which mark *some* units), bold prose headers like `**Sharpness (the exact reachable set).**` and `**Remark (…).**` (which mark others), `[r1]`/`[r2]`/`[r3]` brackets (which mark corrections, sometimes mid-sentence), and long unmarked prose. So every boundary below was **chosen**.

### 6.0 The rule applied

> **A unit is the smallest span carrying one complete claim together with its proof obligations.**

Operationally, that resolved into a four-part test, applied in this order:

1. **Does the span have a proof obligation of its own?** A displayed lemma, a derivation, a case enumeration, or a machine verdict — yes. A pointer sentence, a heading, or a restatement — no (it folds into the unit it points at).
2. **Is the span cited BY DESIGNATION from elsewhere?** `(S5.2)`, `(RES₂)`, `(S5.8.1)`, `(†₂)`, `(‡₂)`, `HE7-13′`, `§S6(iii)` are all cited by name from other spans. A named, separately-cited object is addressable, therefore a unit — even when it is physically inside another unit's proof.
3. **Does the span have its OWN supersession history?** This is the strongest splitter in a note with four post-freeze annex layers. Two clauses of one blockquote that are riddered by different annexes must be separate units, or the CONDITIONALITY field lies about one of them.
4. **Does the span have its own conditionality or its own consumer set?** Two claims with different hypotheses, or with disjoint downstream consumers, split.

Proofs are NOT units: template v2 rule 1 makes DERIVATION a *field*, so §S3, §S4.2's (LIFT₂) proof, §S5.7's proof of HE7.A(1)(2), and §S7's proof of THEOREM HE7.C are all carried inside their statements' units.

### 6.3 Which v2 rules fought the prose genre

Recorded as **format evidence for GRTJB, GRTJC and GENIND** (the remaining tag-sparse prose notes), one line each plus the fix.

1. **CANONICAL STATEMENT "verbatim-or-[ASSEMBLED]" assumes the statement is a display.** In HE7 roughly a third of the units are *bold-prose-headed paragraphs* (`**Sharpness (…).**`, `**Remark (…).**`, `**[r1] TWO ADDITIONS …**`) with no display at all; quoting them verbatim quotes ordinary prose, which is correct but makes "the statement" and "the exposition" typographically indistinguishable in the spec. **Fix: add a `FORM:` sub-field — `display | bold-headed paragraph | bracketed rider | inline sentence` — so a downstream chapter cut can tell a theorem from a remark without re-reading the note.**
2. **The single-span RESOLUTION TRACE fights statement/proof separation.** See §6.1 item 23: statements and proofs are up to 880 lines apart. **Fix: trace takes `statement lines` + `proof lines` + `correction sites`, three slots, not one range.**
3. **The type enum still has no slot for a note's own FINDING-against-a-supplier.** `FINDING HE7-F1` (`.21`) is not a lemma, not an instance record, not a regression record, and not a scope record: it is a *defect report against another note, discharged at the source*. Filed as `[changes-record]` faute de mieux. **Fix: add `supplier-finding` to the enum** — the tagged genre had none of these; prose proof notes have many (HE7 alone carries F1 plus four inbound supplier corrections).
4. **"Tables are units" (rule 5) under-specifies ASCII displays.** §S8's dictionary is a fixed-width ASCII block inside an indented code fence, not a Markdown table. Transcribing it as a Markdown table loses the source bytes; quoting it verbatim violates rule 5. **`.58` does BOTH** (verbatim display, then `[TABLE]` transcription). **Fix: make the double-emission the rule for ASCII tables, explicitly.**
5. **Rule 2 (XREF grep-verification) has no vocabulary for a designation that exists only with different case or punctuation.** Three of HE7's citations fail fixed-string grep purely on capitalisation or a period inside bold (`twist (letters only)`, `finding 2`, `HMENU3-ANNEX-A`). Emitting them as OPEN-CALLs would be over-reporting; suppressing them would lose real DAG edges. **Fix: a `NEAR-MISS` disposition — "designation absent as written; verified referent at `<file>:<line>` under `<actual string>`, count N" — distinct from both a clean XREF and an OPEN-CALL.**
6. **Rule 6 (teeth inverse table) assumes every guarded unit has a tooth.** In a prose proof note the *proof-only* units are the interesting ones. **Fix: require an explicit `PROOF-ONLY` row-class in the inverse table, quoting the note's own coverage-hole sentence** (this shard has exactly one, `.39`, and the note names it).
7. **Rule 7 (scope split) assumed the note has an absorption set.** HE7 absorbs nothing; it *discharges a box* and *receives* four corrections from suppliers. The A/B split still works, but predicate B ("what has been landed on other notes") is answered "nothing, deliberately, three times over". **Fix: allow predicate B to be answered by an explicit NO-APPENDS declaration with its quotes, and add predicate C: inbound corrections received.** Four of this shard's six cross-shard supersessions are inbound supplier corrections, and the v2 header has no place for them.
8. **The rider-vs-supersession distinction is missing and the prose genre is full of riders.** ANNEX F-1's "Read as" (a scope pin: the display stays true, its *wiring* is re-routed), ANNEX R R3/R5's "Rider: read X as Y" (a wording substitution), and M-B's "Read … as …" (a proof-provenance substitution) are three different operations, all phrased identically. **Fix: `SUPERSESSION KIND: replacement | wording-rider | scope-pin | provenance-rider | as-of scoping`** — this shard needed all five.
