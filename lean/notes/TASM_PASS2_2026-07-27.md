## B. Full verification of `(ns-null)`

The proof is sound.

1. **Admissibility of \(Z_M\).**

   Section C defines admissible \(Z\) as a finite digit system whose equations solve for coordinates free on \(\Sigma_k\).

   Here each equation is \(y_{(b,l)}=0\), with:

   - \(b<\mu D\), hence a factor-interior index;
   - \(\operatorname{ht}(b,l)>F(b)\).

   By `(ZC-a)`, interior coordinates are determined exactly through the inclusive top \(\operatorname{ht}\le F(b)\). By `(ZC-b)`, DIG leads occur only at rim indices \(b\ge\mu D\). Therefore every selected coordinate is a non-lead free parameter of the solved graph. Its possible occurrence as a non-lead argument on a DIG right-hand side does not make it determined.

   Thus \(Z_M\) satisfies §C’s actual admissibility definition.

2. **Application of Theorem C(b).**

   No deepening history is needed. One fixes the single realized entrance history \(H\) and appends the finite terminal system \(Z_M\). Theorem C explicitly permits any finite admissible terminal digit system.

   Since these are base \(\mathbf F_p\)-coordinate equations,

   \[
   \operatorname{vol}(Z_M)=p^{-|Z_M|}.
   \]

   Via Theorem C(a)’s transport bijection,

   \[
   \mu(\Sigma\cap L_M)
     =\mu(S(H,Z_M))
     =\mu(S(H,\top))p^{-|Z_M|}
     =\mu(\Sigma)p^{-|Z_M|}.
   \]

3. **Divergence of \(|Z_M|\).**

   The interior is nonempty because \(\mu D\ge1\), so base index \(b=0\) lies in it. At fixed \(b=0\), coordinates occur at every integral base level, and their heights are \(l+\operatorname{off}(0)\), hence unit-spaced. Consequently the number with
   \(F(0)<\operatorname{ht}(0,l)\le M\) grows linearly with \(M\). The displayed lower bound up to harmless floor/initial-index normalization is valid, so \(|Z_M|\to\infty\).

4. **Continuity from above.**

   The sets \(\Sigma\cap L_M\) are measurable digit cylinders, decrease with \(M\), and lie in the normalized coefficient box of finite measure \(1\). Therefore

   \[
   \mu\!\left(\bigcap_M(\Sigma\cap L_M)\right)
   =\lim_M\mu(\Sigma\cap L_M)=0.
   \]

   This is exactly the standard continuity-from-above theorem. The conditional nullity follows because every realized \(\Sigma\) has positive finite cylinder mass.
