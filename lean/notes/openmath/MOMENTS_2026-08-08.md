## S0. What access we now have (the overview — read this one)

The random object: a degree-n polynomial over the p-adic integers O with residue field
F_q, coefficients Haar-uniform. **X_n := the number of its roots in O** (equivalently
the number of (1,1)-factors of its factorization type — degree-1 unramified split
factors). Two normalizations, both standard: the **monic** model (f = x^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1}
+ … + a_0, coefficients uniform) and the **projective** model (a uniform effective divisor
of degree n on P¹, i.e. a random binary form up to scalars; b_n := |Pⁿ(F_q)| =
(q^{n+1}−1)/(q−1) of them). The repo's committed BCFG reference rows are the projective
model. BCFGTIE settled the mean: E[X_n] = q/(q+1) for every n ≥ 2 (monic) and
E[X_n] = 1 exactly (projective). This note settles the rest.

**Level 1 — every fixed n, exactly, from a small recursion.** The full law of X_n is
computable in closed form as a rational function of q, at every n, from ONE input: the
deep-count law P_m(j) of BCFGTIE Lemma C (a two-line q-power formula). No density menu,
no type enumeration, no engine. The mechanism is a probability-generating-function
(PGF) recursion:

      phi_m(t)  =  sum_{j=0..m}  P_m(j) · F_j(t)                    (cluster PGF)
      sum_n F_n(t) u^n      =  Psi(u/q, t)^q     / (1−u)            (monic)
      sum_n b_n F_n^p(t)u^n =  Psi(u,   t)^{q+1} / ((1−u)(1−qu))    (projective)

where F_n(t) = E[t^{X_n}], phi_m is the same for a "cluster" of m roots collapsing to one
residue point, and Psi(v,t) := (1−v)·sum_m phi_m(t)v^m. Read it as: **each of the q
(resp. q+1) rational points of the residue line carries an independent root count; the
point's multiplicity is geometric; the cluster sitting over a multiplicity-m point
re-enters the SAME family one level deeper.** Cost: O(n²) rational-function operations
per degree — degree 10 in seconds, against the exponential type-menu route. This is the
answer to "the entire distribution": yes, at every n, exactly.

Worked example, the whole law at n = 2 (monic):

      F_2(t) = ( q·t² + q + 2 ) / ( 2(q+1) )
      i.e.  P(X_2 = 2) = q/(2(q+1)),  P(X_2 = 1) = 0,  P(X_2 = 0) = (q+2)/(2(q+1)).

The vanishing middle coefficient is a sanity anchor the machine re-checks at every n: a
degree-n polynomial cannot have exactly n−1 roots.

**Level 2 — the moments stabilize, and there is a sum rule.** Write
M_k(n) := E[X_n(X_n−1)···(X_n−k+1)], the k-th factorial moment. The mean's famous
n-independence is the k = 1 case of a general law:

      THEOREM (STABILIZATION).  M_k(n) = M_k(2k)  for every n ≥ 2k,
                                and  M_k(2k−1) ≠ M_k(2k)  (the threshold is exact).
      Projective:  M_k^p(n) = M_k^p(2k)  for every n ≥ 2k, and this common value is
      exactly the k-th factorial moment of the n → ∞ limit law.
      SUM RULE.   sum_{i=0}^{n} q^i M_k(i)  =  b_n · M_k(n)   for every n ≥ 2k,
      equivalently  M_k(2k) = (q−1)/(q^{2k}−1) · sum_{i=k}^{2k−1} q^i M_k(i)
      — the stabilized value is SOLVED by the finitely many lower degrees.

k = 1 recovers BCFGTIE exactly: threshold 2·1 = 2, and the sum rule at n = 2 reads
q·1 + q²·M_1(2) = (q²+q+1)M_1(2), i.e. M_1(2) = q/(q+1). So "the mean is n-independent
past n = 2" and "the variance is n-independent past n = 4" are the same theorem at k = 1
and k = 2. The variance, in closed form (monic):

      M_2(2) = q/(q+1)
      M_2(3) = q³(q²−q+1) / ((q+1)·Phi_5)                       Phi_5 = q⁴+q³+q²+q+1
      M_2(n) = q³(q³+q+1) / ((q+1)²·Phi_5)      for every n ≥ 4
      Var(X_n) = M_2(n) + M_1(n) − M_1(n)² = q³(q³+q+1)/((q+1)²Phi_5) + q/(q+1)²   (n ≥ 4)

At q = 2: M_2(∞) = 8·11/(9·31) = 88/279 ≈ 0.3154, Var(X_n) = 88/279 + 2/9 ≈ 0.5377
against a mean of 2/3 — the count is over-dispersed relative to its mean, i.e. a random
2-adic polynomial of large degree either misses O entirely or picks up a small clump of
roots. The corresponding cluster statement (the object BCFGTIE called eps_m = E[Y_m])
is: **E[(Y_m)_k] is independent of m for m ≥ 2k** — BCFGTIE's "eps_m = 1/(q+1) for all
m ≥ 2" is again the k = 1 case.

**Level 3 — the limiting distribution, in closed form as a product.** As n → ∞ the law
converges, and the limit factorizes over the rational points of the residue line:

      F_inf(t)   =  g(t)^q          (monic, q points of A¹)
      F_inf^p(t) =  g(t)^{q+1}      (projective, q+1 points of P¹ — the PGL_2 form)
      g(t) := Psi(1/q, t) = (1−1/q)·sum_{m≥0} q^{−m} phi_m(t)

g is the PGF of the root count contributed by ONE rational residue disc: the disc's
multiplicity is geometric, P(m) = (1−1/q)q^{−m}, and then the cluster of that size
contributes its own count. Every factorial moment of the limit is an exact rational
function of q — indeed the k-th one is *literally the degree-2k value*: M_k(∞) = M_k(2k).
The individual limiting probabilities P(X_inf = r) are NOT rational functions (they are
q-series with superexponentially convergent tails; explicit bound in S6) — that is the
one place where "closed form" degrades, and it is boxed honestly in S7.

**The one identity that does all the work.** Everything above follows from a single
exponent identity about the deep-count law:

      (INF=GEO)     Psi(1, t)  =  Psi(1/q, t) ,

in words: *a point whose multiplicity is geometric with ratio 1/q has exactly the same
root-count law as a point of infinite multiplicity.* Its weight form is
G(j) = P_inf(j) (S3), proved by a two-line telescope. It generalizes the KEY IDENTITY
q·P_m(0) − P_m(1) = q−1 that carried BCFGTIE's mean; the KEY IDENTITY is the visible
shadow, at k = 1, of the same cancellation. And it is the reason the n-dependence
cancels in the projective moment formula

      M_k^p(n)  =  [ q^{n+1}·D_k^p(1/q) − D_k^p(1) ] / (q^{n+1} − 1)     (n ≥ 2k),

which is constant precisely because D_k^p(1) = D_k^p(1/q).

**What this does NOT rest on.** The whole chain is **HE-free**: no μ≥3 composite-stage
law, none of the [GENHN-HE(μ≥3)] territory. Roots are (1,1)-factors, and (1,1)-factors
live only on integer-slope, unramified-split geometry; the fractional "shallow band"
inside every cluster is root-free by valuation (a root has integer valuation, and the
band's slopes lie strictly in (0,1)), so all of its internal structure — every
composite stage, every μ≥3 stratum — enters only through its total probability mass and
cancels out of the PGF. Stated once, in S8, and used everywhere.

**What is new here vs. BCFGTIE.** BCFGTIE proved the first moment and machine-tied the
n ≤ 6 distribution rows to the committed BCFG transcription. This note (a) lifts the
mean's recursion to the full PGF, (b) proves the stabilization theorem and the sum rule
for every k, (c) identifies the limit law as g^q / g^{q+1}, and (d) supplies the first
in-repo machine check that tests the *level-0 independence* step rather than only
additivity of expectation — tooth T5 (S9) is a mutant in which the q+1 points are
perfectly correlated instead of independent: it reproduces E^p_n = 1 at EVERY n and is
caught only at the second moment.

## S1. Set-up, notation, and the two models

O a complete DVR with maximal ideal (π), residue field F_q, Haar probability on O.

- **Monic model** M_n: f = x^n + a_{n−1}x^{n−1} + … + a_0, (a_i) iid Haar on O.
- **Projective model** P_n: uniform among the b_n := (q^{n+1}−1)/(q−1) effective divisors
  of degree n on P¹_O … i.e. uniform among binary forms of degree n with unit content,
  modulo O*. (The committed BCFG rows are this model; BCFGTIE S7.)
- X_n := #{roots in O} (monic) resp. #{roots in P¹(K)} (projective) = the number of
  (1,1)-factors. Almost surely the discriminant is nonzero, so roots are simple a.s.
- F_n(t) := E[t^{X_n}], F_n^p(t) := E_proj[t^{X_n}]. F_0 = 1, F_1 = t (a linear
  polynomial has exactly one root).
- **Cluster**: given a rational point x of the residue line and a member whose reduction
  has multiplicity exactly m at x, the Hensel factor over x is a *size-m cluster*; its
  root count is Y_m, phi_m(t) := E[t^{Y_m}]. phi_0 = 1, phi_1 = t.
- Psi(v,t) := (1−v)·Σ_{m≥0} phi_m(t) v^m. Note Psi(v,1) = 1 identically.
- d_m := q^{−m(m−1)/2}; b_n as above; (x)_k := x(x−1)···(x−k+1);
  M_k(n) := E[(X_n)_k], M_k^p(n) := E_proj[(X_n)_k], m_k(m) := E[(Y_m)_k].
- P_m(j) := P(exactly j of the m cluster roots have valuation ≥ 1) — BCFGTIE Lemma C.
- Psi_k(v) := ∂_t^k Psi(v,t)|_{t=1} = (1−v)Σ_m m_k(m)v^m (so Psi_k(v) = E[(Y_M)_k] for
  M geometric with P(M=m) = (1−v)v^m — the "per-point k-th factorial moment at rate v").
- D_k(v) := ∂_t^k[Psi(v,t)^q]|_{t=1}, D_k^p(v) := ∂_t^k[Psi(v,t)^{q+1}]|_{t=1}.

## S2. The PGF recursion

### LEMMA 1 (PGF-CLU). For every m ≥ 0:   phi_m(t) = Σ_{j=0}^{m} P_m(j)·F_j(t).

*Proof.* This is BCFGTIE Lemma B read at the level of laws instead of means, and it
consumes exactly the same two facts, which are CITED not re-proved: (i) recentre the
cluster at 0 and split its Newton polygon at slope 1 — the *shallow band* (root
valuations in (0,1)) is root-free because a root of a polynomial over O has integer
valuation, so it contributes 0 to X, deterministically, whatever its internal structure;
(ii) conditionally on the deep count being j, the deep factor, rescaled by x ↦ πy, is a
Haar-uniform monic polynomial of degree j (BCFGTIE Lemma B step 2 = the W-12.D /
GENIND.B integer-slope cell law, the α-species self-similar re-entry). Given (i)+(ii),
Y_m = X_j^{(deep)} conditionally on the deep count j, so E[t^{Y_m}] = Σ_j P_m(j)E[t^{X_j}]
with no independence needed. ∎

### LEMMA 2 (PGF-MON / PGF-PRJ).  As identities of formal power series in u:

      (MON)  Σ_{n≥0} F_n(t) u^n      =  Psi(u/q, t)^q     / (1 − u)
      (PRJ)  Σ_{n≥0} b_n F_n^p(t)u^n =  Psi(u,   t)^{q+1} / ((1 − u)(1 − qu))

*Proof.* Level-0: the reduction of a random member of M_n is uniform on the q^n monic
degree-n polynomials over F_q (resp. of P_n uniform on the b_n degree-n effective
divisors on P¹_{F_q}). Factor the reduction into closed points and lift by Hensel:
f = Π_x f_x over the closed points x of the support, with deg f_x = m_x·deg(x). Two
inputs, both cited:

- **(ADD)** X is additive over this factorization, and a closed point of residue degree
  ≥ 2 contributes 0 (its factor is ≡ 0 mod a degree-≥2 irreducible, so carries no
  (1,1)); a rational point of multiplicity m contributes Y_m. [BCFGTIE Lemma A.]
- **(J-D0-PROD)** conditionally on the level-0 reduction, the Hensel factors f_x are
  INDEPENDENT, each Haar-conditioned on its own reduction; i.e. the map f ↦ (f_x)_x is a
  measure isomorphism onto the product of the conditional Haar measures. [The level-0
  product / J-D0 argument, accepted in the weld — the same supplier BCFGTIE's Lemma A
  cites for "independent of the rest (Hensel/cell independence at order 0)". BCFGTIE
  needed only the marginals, because expectation is additive; the PGF needs the product,
  which is what J-D0 supplies. This is the one place the PGF asks strictly more than
  the mean, and it is exactly what tooth T5 tests.]

Given (ADD)+(J-D0-PROD), E[t^{X_n} | reduction] = Π_{x rational} phi_{m_x}(t). Now count
with an Euler product over closed points. Let W(u) := Σ_{m≥0} phi_m(t)u^m. Over A¹ there
are q rational points and Π_{all x}(1−u^{deg x})^{−1} = (1−qu)^{−1}, so the non-rational
part is Π_{deg ≥ 2}(1−u^{deg x})^{−1} = (1−u)^q/(1−qu) and

      Σ_n u^n Σ_{f̄ monic deg n} Π_x phi_{m_x} = W(u)^q · (1−u)^q/(1−qu)
                                               = Psi(u,t)^q/(1−qu).

Dividing by q^n (uniformity) is u ↦ u/q, and 1/(1−qu) ↦ 1/(1−u): that is (MON).
Over P¹ there are q+1 rational points and Π_{all x}(1−u^{deg x})^{−1} = Z_{P¹}(u) =
1/((1−u)(1−qu)) = Σ_n b_n u^n, so the non-rational part is again (1−u)^q/(1−qu) and

      Σ_n u^n Σ_{divisors deg n} Π_x phi_{m_x} = W(u)^{q+1}(1−u)^q/(1−qu)
                                               = Psi(u,t)^{q+1}/((1−u)(1−qu)),

which is (PRJ) (no rescaling: the projective count b_n is already on the left). ∎

**Sanity, at t = 1.** Psi(v,1) = 1, so (MON) collapses to Σ_n u^n = 1/(1−u) and (PRJ) to
Σ_n b_n u^n = 1/((1−u)(1−qu)): both master identities are normalization-exact.

**Corollary 2.1 (the recursion is well-founded and determines everything).** Writing
Psi = 1 + Σ_{m≥1}psi_m(t)v^m with psi_m = phi_m − phi_{m−1}, the v^n coefficient of Psi^q
is q·psi_n + (products of psi_j, j<n). Hence, via Lemma 1, F_n satisfies a LINEAR
equation whose self-coefficient is q^{1−n}·q^{−n(n−1)/2} < 1 for n ≥ 2 (BCFGTIE's
contraction), so F_n is uniquely determined by F_0,…,F_{n−1}. Only P_m(j) is needed.
Cost: O(n²) truncated-series products per degree.

**Corollary 2.2 (the point at infinity).** (MON) and (PRJ) give, algebraically,
b_n F_n^p = Σ_{j=0}^{n} q^{n−j} F_{n−j} phi_j — "a random degree-n divisor on P¹ is a
random monic polynomial of degree n−j on the affine chart together with an independent
cluster of size j at infinity, where P(j) = q^{n−j}/b_n". The limiting form is
F_inf^p = g·F_inf (one extra point), consistent with S5.

## S3. The three exponent laws of the deep-count law

Fix m ≥ 2 and use BCFGTIE Lemma C's parametrization: u_i := v(a_i)−1 ≥ 0 (i < m), iid
with P(u ≥ s) = q^{−s}; t_i := i + u_i + 1, t_m := m; the deep count is
j = max argmin_i t_i. Write c(j,μ) := P(min_i t_i = μ and the max-argmin is j), for
0 ≤ j < μ. Then, exactly as in BCFGTIE Lemma C,

      P_m(j) = Σ_{μ=j+1}^{m−1} c(j,μ)   (j < m),        P_m(m) = d_m = q^{−m(m−1)/2},

and — this is the point — **c(j,μ) does not depend on m**: the m-dependence of the law is
entirely in the RANGE of μ. Explicitly, c(j,μ) = (1−1/q)·q^{−E(j,μ)} with
E(j,μ) = (μ−j−1) + j(μ−1) − j(j−1)/2 + (μ−1−j)(μ−j)/2.

### LEMMA 3 (GEOM-J).  c(j,μ) = q^j · c(0,μ)  for all 0 ≤ j < μ.

*Proof.* E(j,μ) − E(j−1,μ) = −1 + (μ−1) − (j−1) − (μ−j) = −1, independent of j and μ. ∎

*(The KEY IDENTITY of BCFGTIE is the case j = 1: q·c(0,μ) = c(1,μ) for every μ ≥ 2 makes
q·P_m(0) − P_m(1) = q−1 collapse to its μ = 1 term. BCFGTIE proved that one instance by
"the exponents are identical, so the sums cancel pairwise"; Lemma 3 is the whole family.)*

### LEMMA 4 (MASS).  Σ_{j<μ} c(j,μ) = d_μ − d_{μ+1}.

*Proof.* By Lemma 3 the left side is c(0,μ)(q^μ−1)/(q−1) =
q^{−μ(μ−1)/2}(1−q^{−μ}) = d_μ − d_{μ+1} by direct exponent arithmetic. (Equivalently:
sum Σ_j P_m(j) = 1 over m and telescope.) ∎

### LEMMA 5 (INF=GEO).   Psi(1, t) = Psi(1/q, t).

Both sides are honest PGFs of N-valued variables: Psi(1/q,t) = (1−1/q)Σ_m q^{−m}phi_m(t)
=: g(t) is the mixture of cluster laws over a geometric multiplicity
P(m) = (1−1/q)q^{−m}, and Psi(1,t) = lim_{v→1}(1−v)Σ_m phi_m(t)v^m = lim_m phi_m(t)
=: phi_inf(t) (Abel), the *infinite-cluster* law. In mixture-weight form, with
P_inf(j) := lim_m P_m(j) = Σ_{μ>j}c(j,μ) and G(j) := (1−1/q)Σ_m q^{−m}P_m(j):

      G(j) = P_inf(j)   for every j ≥ 0,   hence   g = Σ_j G(j)F_j = Σ_j P_inf(j)F_j = phi_inf.

*Proof of G(j) = P_inf(j).* Summing the m-expansion, G(j) = Σ_{μ>j}c(j,μ)q^{−μ−1} +
(1−1/q)q^{−j}d_j, so the claim is
q^j Σ_{μ>j} c(0,μ)(1 − q^{−μ−1}) = (1−1/q) q^{−j} d_j (using Lemma 3). Put
ℓ(μ) := μ(μ+1)/2. Then c(0,μ)/(1−1/q) = q^{1−ℓ(μ)} and c(0,μ)q^{−μ−1}/(1−1/q) =
q^{−μ−ℓ(μ)} = q^{1−ℓ(μ+1)}, so the sum telescopes:
Σ_{μ>j}[q^{1−ℓ(μ)} − q^{1−ℓ(μ+1)}] = q^{1−ℓ(j+1)}. Multiplying by q^j(1−1/q):
LHS = (1−1/q)q^{j+1−(j+1)(j+2)/2} = (1−1/q)q^{−j(j+1)/2} = (1−1/q)q^{−j}d_j = RHS,
since −j(j+1)/2 = −j − j(j−1)/2. ∎

**Corollary 5.1.**  D_k(1) = D_k(1/q) and D_k^p(1) = D_k^p(1/q) for every k ≥ 1
(differentiate Psi(1,t)^q = Psi(1/q,t)^q k times at t = 1).

**Corollary 5.2 (Σ P_inf = 1).** Σ_j P_inf(j) = Σ_μ Σ_{j<μ} c(j,μ) = Σ_μ(d_μ−d_{μ+1})
= d_1 = 1, so phi_inf is a genuine PGF.

## S4. THEOREM (moment stabilization, the sum rule, the projective constancy)

For every k ≥ 1:

  (a) Psi_k(v) is a POLYNOMIAL in v of degree ≤ 2k; equivalently m_k(m) = m_k(2k) for
      all m ≥ 2k ("the k-th factorial moment of a cluster's root count does not depend
      on the cluster size once the size is at least 2k").
  (b) M_k(n) = M_k(2k) for every n ≥ 2k, and M_k(2k) = D_k(1/q) = lim_n M_k(n).
  (c) M_k^p(n) = D_k^p(1/q) for every n ≥ 2k — exactly constant, equal to the limit.
  (d) SUM RULE: Σ_{i=0}^{n} q^i M_k(i) = b_n M_k(n) for every n ≥ 2k; equivalently
      M_k(2k) = (q−1)/(q^{2k}−1)·Σ_{i=k}^{2k−1} q^i M_k(i).

**Step 1 (the exact coefficient formula).** By Lemma 1 and the S3 decomposition,

      Psi(v,t) = Σ_{μ≥1} c(0,μ)·W(μ)(t)·v^{μ+1}  +  (1−v)Σ_{m≥0} d_m F_m(t) v^m,
      W(μ)(t) := Σ_{j<μ} q^j F_j(t)      [Lemma 3]

(swap the μ- and m-sums: Σ_{m>μ}v^m = v^{μ+1}/(1−v) supplies the 1/(1−v) that cancels
the prefactor). Differentiating k times at t = 1, with T_k(μ) := Σ_{i<μ} q^i M_k(i):

      [v^N] Psi_k  =  c(0,N−1)·T_k(N−1)  +  d_N M_k(N)  −  d_{N−1} M_k(N−1).      (★)

**Step 2 (entirety).** M_k(n) ≤ n^k and Σ_{i<μ}q^i ≤ q^μ/(q−1), while c(0,μ) and d_μ
decay like q^{−Θ(μ²)}; so both series in Step 1 have infinite radius: Psi_k, hence
D_k = q·Psi_k + R_k and D_k^p, are ENTIRE in v with absolutely summable coefficients at
v = 1. In particular M_k(n) = Σ_{i≤n} q^{−i}[v^i]D_k converges to M_k^∞ := D_k(1/q).
(Here R_k := Σ_{π ⊢ [k], |π| ≥ 2} (q)_{|π|} Π_{B∈π} Psi_{|B|}, from the Faà-di-Bruno
expansion D_k = Σ_{π⊢[k]}(q)_{|π|}Π_B Psi_{|B|} of ∂_t^k(Psi^q) at Psi = 1; the single
block gives q·Psi_k, and every π with |π| ≥ 2 has all blocks of size < k.)

**Step 3 (the sum rule holds as a convergent identity — unconditionally).** With
e_i := M_k(i) − M_k^∞ = −Σ_{j>i} q^{−j}[v^j]D_k,

      Σ_{i≥0} q^i e_i = −Σ_j q^{−j}[v^j]D_k · (q^j−1)/(q−1) = −[D_k(1) − D_k(1/q)]/(q−1) = 0

by Corollary 5.1 (all rearrangements absolutely convergent by Step 2). Call this
Δ_k = 0. **This is where (INF=GEO) enters, and it is the whole content of the theorem.**

**Step 4 ((a)+(b), induction on k).** Assume Psi_j is a polynomial of degree ≤ 2j for
j < k (base k = 1: R_1 = 0 and Psi_1(v) = v − qv²/(q+1) directly from m_1(0)=0, m_1(1)=1,
m_1(m)=1/(q+1) (m≥2) — BCFGTIE's eps_m). Then R_k is a polynomial of degree ≤ Σ_B 2|B|
= 2k, so for n > 2k we have [v^n]D_k = q[v^n]Psi_k and

      e_n − e_{n−1} = q^{−n}[v^n]D_k = q^{1−n}[v^n]Psi_k .

Feed (★) into this, using T_k(n−1) = Σ_{i<n−1}q^i e_i + M_k^∞(q^{n−1}−1)/(q−1),
c(0,n−1)(q^{n−1}−1)/(q−1) = d_{n−1}−d_n (Lemma 4) and Δ_k = 0 (so

## S5. The limiting law

By (MON), F_n = Σ_{i≤n}[u^i]{Psi(u/q,t)^q}, a partial sum, so F_n(t) → F_inf(t) :=
Psi(1/q,t)^q = g(t)^q for every t ∈ [0,1]. The rate is best stated as an exact coupling,
via a small counting identity worth recording:

### LEMMA 5.1 (the level-0 profile is EXACTLY iid geometric on the bulk).
Let ν_d := (density of monic degree-d polynomials over F_q with no rational root). Then
Σ_d ν_d q^d u^d = (1−u)^q/(1−qu) (the Euler product with all rational points forced to
multiplicity 0), whence ν_d = Σ_{s} C(q,s)(−1)^s q^{−s} = **(1−1/q)^q exactly, for every
d ≥ q**. Consequently, for a uniform monic f̄ of degree n over F_q, the multiplicity
profile (m_x)_{x ∈ F_q} at the rational points has
P(profile) = q^{−Σm}·ν_{n−Σm} = (1−1/q)^q q^{−Σm}, i.e. **exactly the law of q iid
variables M_x with P(M = m) = (1−1/q)q^{−m}**, for every profile with Σ_x m_x ≤ n − q.

Since X = Σ_x Y_{m_x} with the same conditional cluster laws in both models, the two laws
of X agree except on the event {Σ_x M_x > n − q}, so

      d_TV( law(X_n) , law(X_inf) )  ≤  P( M_1 + … + M_q > n − q )
                                     =  (1−1/q)^q Σ_{s>n−q} C(s+q−1, q−1) q^{−s}
                                     =  O( n^{q−1} q^{−n} ) .

Likewise for the projective model with q+1 points (Step 5's formula gives it directly:
F_n^p(t) = [q^{n+1}D_0^p(1/q) − D_0^p(1)]/(q^{n+1}−1)-type expressions in each t-degree).
So

      **F_inf(t) = g(t)^q ,   F_inf^p(t) = g(t)^{q+1} ,   g(t) = (1−1/q)Σ_{m≥0} q^{−m}phi_m(t)
      = phi_inf(t)  (Lemma 5).**

Interpretation: in the limit the q (resp. q+1) rational residue discs decouple into iid
contributions; each disc's multiplicity is geometric with ratio 1/q, and by (INF=GEO)
this is the same as saying each disc carries an infinite cluster. All factorial moments
of the limit are exact rational functions of q, namely M_k(∞) = M_k(2k) and
M_k^p(∞) = M_k^p(2k) (S4(b),(c)). The individual probabilities are
P(X_inf = r) = [t^r]g^q, and since [t^r]g = Σ_{j≥r}P_inf(j)[t^r]F_j is an infinite sum
with weights P_inf(j) = q^jΣ_{μ>j}c(0,μ) = Θ(q^{−j(j+1)/2}), they are convergent

already give ~q^{−45}. Since X_n ≤ n, the moments determine the law:
P(X_inf = r) = Σ_{k≥r}(−1)^{k−r}M_k(∞)/(r!(k−r)!) — the limiting law is determined by the
rational-function sequence {M_k(2k)}_k.
