## S2. THE MECHANISM, AND WHY THE μ = 2 ARGUMENT STOPS AT μ = 2

**The two-line mechanism.** (1) Every root ρ of f satisfies the same
two transported facts (T1)/(T2) as a root of Φ′, so by the SLOT LEMMA
the polynomial relation satisfied by y_ρ := Φ′(ρ) over L_ρ := K₀(ρ),
namely Y^μ + Σ_j A_j(ρ)Y^j = 0, has *exactly the stage polygon* and
*Frobenius-conjugates of the stage residuals*; hence each root of f
carries a LABEL (λ, r), and its label forces e₁e_s | e(L_ρ) and
f₁f_s | f(L_ρ). (2) The label CLASS SIZES are pinned by the family of
resultant valuations v(Res(f, Φ′_t)) over test keys Φ′_t = Φ′ + ŝ·n(κ)
— the ultrametric distance function from f's root multiset to the test
points — and they come out to exactly D′e_sf_s, whereupon the degree
count Σ_labels D′e_sf_s = D′μ = deg f forces each class to be a single
Galois orbit with e = e₁e_s, f = f₁f_s exactly.

**Why GENHN.C's μ = 2 argument stops.** GENHN.C (§S7 there) reads the
Y-quadratic and closes each case with ONE of two devices: (a) the label
set is a SINGLETON (stage-RAM: one side, ℓ = 2, u odd; stage-INERT: one
side, residual irreducible of degree 2), in which case all D′μ roots
carry that label and no counting is needed; or (b) the label set has
exactly TWO members (stage-2SIDED, and stage-SPLITEQ after one refine
step reduces it to 2SIDED), in which case the two class sizes n₁, n₂
are determined by the two linear equations

    n₁ + n₂ = D′μ ,      n₁λ₁ + n₂λ₂ = D′·dv(A₀)

(root count + the single resultant identity Σ_ρ dv(Φ′(ρ)) = D′dv(A₀)),
a 2 × 2 system with λ₁ ≠ λ₂, hence invertible.

At μ = 2 those are the only possibilities: Σ e_sf_s = 2 admits only
{(2,1)}, {(1,2)}, {(1,1),(1,1)}. **At μ ≥ 3 the label set can have
THREE or more members** — Σ e_sf_s = 3 admits {(1,1),(1,1),(1,1)} —
and then the two identities are underdetermined; worse, if the three
labels sit on the SAME side (one slope λ, residual = three distinct
K-rational roots) the second equation degenerates to λ times the
first and carries **zero** information. That is the exact failure, and
it is the first-live μ = 3 configuration at n = 6 (a sextic splitting
into three ramified quadratics when e₁ = 2, f₁ = 1). Battery tooth
HE-T-UNDET certifies the underdetermination numerically.

**What replaces it.** The single identity is replaced by the whole
FAMILY of identities indexed by test keys: define, for integer κ and
a letter s ∈ K^×,

    Ψ(κ, s) := Σ_ρ dv(Φ′_{κ,s}(ρ)) = D′·dv_t(f mod Φ′_{κ,s})
    where Φ′_{κ,s} := Φ′ + ŝ·n(κ)   (n(κ) = the dv-height-κ normalizer).

For s generic at height κ (not a residual root of the height-κ side)
Ψ(κ, s) = D′·min_j(dv(A_j) + jκ) — the polygon's support function
scaled by D′ — and on the root side Ψ(κ, s) = Σ_labels n_λ·min(λ, κ).
Sampling κ over the integers inverts (LEMMA HE3-3) to give the slope
counts n_λ = D′L_λ; recentering at each K-rational residual root and
re-running the same inversion peels the residue classes (LEMMA HE3-4),
with unramified base change supplying letters for the non-rational
residual factors. Two features of this replacement are worth naming:
it is *characteristic-free* (no binomial expansion is used — only the
substitution identity for residual polynomials), and it is *degree-
generic* (nothing in it caps μ), which is why the same argument closes
the whole family rather than one more case.

## S3. LEMMA HE3-1 (the resultant identity at every test key)

> **LEMMA HE3-1.** Let Φ″ ∈ O[x] be monic of degree D′ with Φ″ ≡ Φ′
> modulo terms of dv-height > S (so Φ″ is again irreducible with
> e = e₁, f = f₁, roots satisfying (T1)/(T2), and R″ := O[x]/(Φ″) is
> again a complete DVR with residue field K — GENHN-4's invariant
> maintenance, proved there without the graded frame). Write
> f ≡ B₀ mod Φ″ with deg B₀ < D′. Then
>
>     Σ_{ρ : f(ρ) = 0} dv(Φ″(ρ)) = D′ · dv(B₀)        (HE3-1a)
>
> and B₀(θ″) = F(−w)(θ″) where w := Φ″ − Φ′ and F is the stage
> resolvent of DEFINITION 1, θ″ any root of Φ″. Consequently the left
> side is computable from the stage read data alone.

*Proof.* Both f and Φ″ are monic, so
Res(f, Φ″) = Π_ρ Φ″(ρ) = ± Π_{θ″} f(θ″) = ± Nm_{R″/O}(B₀(θ″)) (the
second equality is the standard resultant symmetry; the third is
f(θ″) = B₀(θ″)). Taking v: Σ_ρ v(Φ″(ρ)) = v(Nm B₀(θ″)) =
D′·v(B₀(θ″)) = f₁·dv(B₀). Multiply by e₁ to convert the left side to
dv: Σ_ρ dv(Φ″(ρ)) = e₁f₁·dv(B₀) = D′·dv(B₀). For the second clause,
Φ′ ≡ −w mod Φ″, so f = Φ′^μ + ΣA_jΦ′^j ≡ (−w)^μ + ΣA_j(−w)^j = F(−w)
mod Φ″. ∎

**Remark (no refine transfer).** LEMMA HE3-1 uses only the resultant
symmetry and the norm; the *value* dv(B₀) = dv(F(−w)) is then an
ultrametric evaluation of F at an element of dv-height κ, computed by
the SLOT LEMMA (exact dv(A_j)) plus the residual substitution identity
of §S5. No digit-space bijection, hence no W-9 face.

> **LEMMA HE3-1′ (the evaluation display).** Let κ > S be an integer,
> s ∈ K^×, ŝ ∈ O a lift, n(κ) = x^{i}π^{a} the dv-height-κ normalizer
> (i·h + e₁a = κ, 0 ≤ i < e₁), w := ŝ·n(κ), Φ″ := Φ′ + w. Then with
> B₀ := f mod Φ″,
>
>     dv(B₀) = min_{0 ≤ j ≤ μ} ( dv(A_j) + jκ )                (generic)
>
> whenever κ is NOT a slope of P(F), and also whenever κ IS a slope λ of
> P(F) but s is NOT a root of the residual polynomial R_λ; and
> dv(B₀) > min_j(dv(A_j) + jκ) exactly when κ = λ is a slope and
> R_λ(s) = 0 (with the excess governed by the multiplicity, LEMMA
> HE3-2).

*Proof.* By LEMMA HE3-1, B₀(θ″) = F(−w)(θ″) = Σ_j A_j(θ″)·(−w(θ″))^j.
Each summand has dv exactly dv(A_j) + jκ: dv(A_j(θ″)) = dv(A_j) by the
SLOT LEMMA at θ″ (which satisfies (T1)/(T2) — invariant maintenance),
and dv(w(θ″)) = κ. By the ultrametric inequality dv(B₀) ≥ min_j(…),
with equality unless the minimum is attained at ≥ 2 indices AND their
residues cancel. Group the indices attaining the minimum: they are
exactly the j on the height-κ supporting line of P(F), i.e. the lattice
points of the side of slope κ if κ is a slope, and a single vertex
otherwise. In the former case the sum of the residues of the attaining
summands is, after dividing by the common normalizer n(min) and folding
the normalizer wrap through η (S4 of GENHN, the cocycle
n(κ)^t = ẑ^{fl}n(tκ)), precisely R_λ(s) up to a nonzero K-factor;
so cancellation ⟺ R_λ(s) = 0. In the latter case a single summand
attains the minimum and no cancellation is possible. ∎

**Remark (what the twist does and does not do).** The wrap factors
ẑ^{fl} are nonzero constants of K depending only on (κ, t, e₁, h); they
rescale the coefficients of R_λ by a fixed nonzero diagonal, hence
change neither its root set up to that rescaling nor its factorization
TYPE. All uses below consult only (a) whether R_λ(s) = 0 and (b) the
degrees of the irreducible factors of R_λ, both twist-invariant. This
is why the battery can detect residual roots by measuring dv(B₀) — a
twist-free measurement — instead of constructing R_λ.

*Proof sketch (completed at commit 2).* (T1)/(T2): the opening locus
is by definition the locus where the ambient polygon has the side of

## S5. LEMMA HE3-2 (recentering: valuations and residuals, elementarily)

> with residual R_λ and s is a root of R_λ of multiplicity m ≥ 1, then
> the portion of P(F″) of slope > λ has horizontal length exactly
> m·ℓ = m (ℓ = 1 for an integer slope λ), the portion of slope λ has
> length reduced by m, and the residual of the slope-λ side of P(F″) is
> R_λ(Z + s)/Z^m up to the twist rescaling; all other sides are
> unchanged;
> (c) [IN-WINDOW, UPWARD] every new digit consulted lies strictly inside
> its class's window and is determined by old digits at weakly lower
> dv-heights (GENHN-3(c)(d)); the key invariants of S3.1 — the constant
> term's valuation f₁h and the residual η — are untouched, since
> dv(w) = κ > S = e₁h places w strictly above the key's own side.

> statements about valuations and residues only.** Both characteristics:

*Proof.* Φ′ = Φ″ − w, so
f = Σ_i A_i(Φ″ − w)^i = Σ_j Φ″^j·[ Σ_{i ≥ j} binom(i, j)(−w)^{i−j}A_i ],

GENHN-3(d), because Φ″ is monic with all lower coefficients of positive
dv). Each summand has dv exactly dv(A_i) + (i−j)κ by the SLOT LEMMA and
dv(w) = κ, giving (a). [The binomial coefficients may vanish in small
characteristic; that only removes summands, so the inequality in (a) —
and the identification of the cancellation locus in (b) via the
substitution identity — are unaffected. This is the point at which an
expansion-based argument would need a characteristic case split and the
substitution-based argument does not: the residual-level content of the
displayed sum is the single identity R_λ(Z + s), evaluated in K.]

For (b): the height of P(F″) at abscissa j is dv(A_j′). Off the
height-κ line the minima in (a) are attained at one index and equality
holds, which reproduces the truncation statement. On the height-κ line,
the residues of the attaining summands assemble (after dividing by the

immediate. (c) is GENHN-3(c)(d) verbatim plus dv(w) = κ > S. ∎

### S6.1 LEMMA HE3-3 (the slope counts)

For an integer κ > S and s ∈ K^× put Φ′_{κ,s} := Φ′ + ŝ·n(κ) and

    Ψ(κ, s) := Σ_{ρ : f(ρ) = 0} dv(Φ′_{κ,s}(ρ))  ∈ ℤ.

> **LEMMA HE3-3.** Let n_λ := #{ρ : dv(Φ′(ρ)) = λ} for λ a slope of
> P(F) (LEMMA HE3-0(iii) guarantees every root's dv is a slope). Then
> **n_λ = D′·L_λ** for every slope λ, where L_λ is the horizontal
> length of the λ-side of P(F).

*Proof.* Three steps.

*(1) The stage side.* By LEMMA HE3-1 and HE3-1′, for every κ > S and
every s that is not a residual root at height κ,

    Ψ(κ, s) = D′·dv(B₀^{(κ,s)}) = D′·min_j( dv(A_j) + jκ )  =: D′·h_F(κ),

so Ψ(κ, s) is independent of such s; call it Ψ(κ). (If K^× happens to
contain no non-root at height κ — possible only when κ is a slope and
R_λ has |K^×| distinct roots — omit that κ from the sample; the argument
below needs only the integers κ that are NOT slopes of P(F), of which
there are arbitrarily many.) Note h_F is the support function of P(F):
by elementary convex duality,

    h_F(κ) = min_j (dv(A_j) + jκ) = Σ_{slopes λ} L_λ · min(λ, κ) .   (★)

[Proof of (★): both sides are concave piecewise-linear in κ. For
κ ≥ max slope both equal Σ_λ L_λλ = dv(A₀) (left side: the minimum is at
j = 0). For κ ≤ min slope both equal μκ (left side: minimum at j = μ,
where dv(A_μ) = 0). Between consecutive slopes both are affine with the
same increment: d/dκ of the right side is Σ_{λ ≥ κ} L_λ, and the left
side's minimising index j(κ) is the abscissa of the vertex where the
supporting line of slope κ touches P(F), i.e. exactly Σ_{λ ≥ κ}L_λ. ∎]

*(2) The root side.* Fix κ > S not a slope, and any s. For each root ρ
with dv(Φ′(ρ)) = λ we have dv(Φ′_{κ,s}(ρ)) = dv(Φ′(ρ) + w(ρ)) =
min(λ, κ) exactly, since λ ≠ κ and dv(w(ρ)) = κ (dv(w(ρ)) = κ because
ρ satisfies (T1), so dv(n(κ)(ρ)) = κ). Summing over roots,

    Ψ(κ) = Σ_λ n_λ · min(λ, κ) .                                   (★★)

*(3) Inversion.* Comparing (★) with (★★) via step (1):

    Σ_λ ( n_λ − D′L_λ ) · min(λ, κ) = 0   for every non-slope integer
    κ > S.

The finitely many slopes λ are known and distinct. Sampling κ over any
set of ≥ #slopes + 1 non-slope integers spanning the slope range, the
functions κ ↦ min(λ, κ) for distinct λ are linearly independent (for
λ₁ < λ₂ a relation c₁min(λ₁,κ) + c₂min(λ₂,κ) ≡ 0 forces c₁ + c₂ = 0 by
the small-κ regime, where both are κ, and then c₁λ₁ + c₂λ₂ = 0 in the
large-κ regime, i.e. c₁(λ₁ − λ₂) = 0, so c₁ = c₂ = 0; the general case
is the same triangular argument on the breakpoints, taking κ in the
integer gaps, which exist because two distinct slopes with denominators
≤ μ differ by ≥ 1/μ² and we may sample κ over a full integer interval
containing all slopes — see the Remark). Hence n_λ = D′L_λ. ∎

**Remark (integer sampling suffices).** Only the values of the concave
function at integers are available (normalizers exist at integer
dv-heights only). Independence at integer samples is what was just
proved: for each pair of distinct slopes there is an integer κ with
min(λ₁, κ) ≠ min(λ₂, κ) up to the common-κ regime, and the triangular
elimination proceeds from the largest slope downward. Concretely, with
κ₀ := ⌊min slope⌋ and κ₁ := ⌈max slope⌉, the (κ₁ − κ₀ + 2) samples
κ ∈ {κ₀, …, κ₁ + 1} (dropping the slopes themselves) already determine
all n_λ, since the second difference of Ψ at κ is supported on the
slopes.

### S6.2 LEMMA HE3-4 (the residue peel)

> **LEMMA HE3-4.** Fix a slope λ and write R_λ = Π_r r^{m_r} (r
> irreducible over K); at a DECIDED leaf all m_r = 1. Let, for each r,
> S_r := {ρ : dv(Φ′(ρ)) = λ and res(Φ′(ρ)/n(λ)(ρ)) is a root of r}.
> Then |S_r| = D′·ℓ·deg r = D′·e_s·f_s. (For ℓ = e_s ≥ 2 the classes
> S_r are indexed by the residual of the λ-side in its own lattice
> normalization; see the proof.)

*Proof.* First assume λ is an integer (ℓ = 1) and r = Z − s is linear
with s ∈ K^×. Apply LEMMA HE3-3 to the recentered key Φ″ = Φ′_{λ,s}:
its own polygon P(F″) is described by LEMMA HE3-2(b), and its slope
counts are D′ times its side lengths. Now for a root ρ,
dv(Φ″(ρ)) > λ ⟺ [dv(Φ′(ρ)) = λ and res(Φ′(ρ)/n(λ)(ρ)) = s] or
dv(Φ′(ρ)) > λ. Hence

    #{ρ : dv(Φ″(ρ)) > λ} − #{ρ : dv(Φ′(ρ)) > λ} = |S_{Z−s}| ,

and by LEMMA HE3-3 applied at both keys the left side is
D′·(length of P(F″) above λ) − D′·(length of P(F) above λ) = D′·m_s =
D′ by LEMMA HE3-2(b) and m_s = 1. So |S_{Z−s}| = D′ = D′·1·1. ✓

Next, general (ℓ, deg r) — two reductions.
(i) *Non-rational residual factors: unramified base change.* Let c ≥ 1
and let O₁/O be the unramified extension with residue field
F_{Q^{c}}; it is again a complete DVR with the same uniformizer π and
value group, Φ′ remains irreducible over O₁ or splits into unramified-
conjugate factors — in either case the SET of roots of f in the fixed
algebraic closure, the valuations dv, the polygon P(F), and every label
are UNCHANGED (only the coefficient field of the residual polynomials
grows, from K to K·F_{Q^c}). Choose c divisible by every deg r and by
enough to make |K·F_{Q^c}|^× > μ; over O₁ every residual root is
rational and the previous paragraph gives |S_{Z−β}| = D′ for each of
the deg r roots β of r. Since S_r = ⊔_{β | r} S_{Z−β} as sets of roots
of f (the same sets before and after base change), |S_r| = D′·deg r. ✓
(ii) *Ramified sides (ℓ ≥ 2).* Here the λ-side's residual lives on the
lattice of abscissae ≡ j₁ mod ℓ and no integer-height normalizer meets
it; the substitute is again a base change, this time RAMIFIED: adjoin
π^{1/ℓ}′ — formally, replace O by O₂ := O[Π]/(Π^ℓ − π), a complete DVR
with the same residue field and dv scaled by ℓ. Over O₂ the slope λ
becomes the integer ℓλ = u in the rescaled ladder, the λ-side's
residual becomes the residual of an integer-slope side of the SAME
degree d_λ, and (i) applies, giving the class sizes ℓ-fold rescaled:
|S_r| = D′·ℓ·deg r. Again the root set and the labels of f are
unchanged by the base change (only the ambient value group is refined),
so the count transfers verbatim to O. ✓

The three displayed reductions are compatible (base changes commute and
neither touches the root set), which finishes the proof. ∎

**Consistency check.** Σ_{λ} Σ_{r | R_λ} D′ℓ_λ deg r = D′ Σ_λ ℓ_λ d_λ
= D′ Σ_λ L_λ = D′μ = deg f. So the label classes exactly partition the
roots — no root is unaccounted and none is double-counted.

### S6.3 PROOF OF THEOREM HE3.A

Let ℓ be a decided leaf and f a lift with disc(f) ≠ 0. Induct on the
number of stage-α refines and stage-CS events in ℓ's history; the
statement to prove at each stage is the displayed dictionary.

*Base (depth-0 decided read).* The stage type is
τ = {(ℓ_λ, deg r)}_{(λ, r)} over the labels of P(F). By LEMMA HE3-0 every
root of f carries a label; by LEMMA HE3-4 the class of each label has
exactly D′·e_s·f_s elements; by the consistency check the classes
partition the D′μ roots. Each class S_{λ,r} is stable under
Gal(K̄₀/K₀): Galois preserves dv and acts on residues through
k̄-automorphisms, and it permutes the roots of the irreducible r, so it
permutes ⊔_{β|r}S_{Z−β} = S_{λ,r}. By COROLLARY HE3-0′ each ρ ∈ S_{λ,r}
has [K₀(ρ) : K₀] ≥ e₁ℓ_λ·f₁ deg r = D′e_sf_s = |S_{λ,r}|. A Galois-stable
set of size N all of whose elements have orbit size ≥ N is a single
orbit of size exactly N. Hence S_{λ,r} is the root set of a single monic
irreducible factor of f, of degree D′e_sf_s, and its e ≥ e₁e_s,
f ≥ f₁f_s with ef = degree forces

    e = e₁·e_s ,   f = f₁·f_s   exactly.

Doing this for every label gives σ(f) = {(e₁e_s^{(i)}, f₁f_s^{(i)})}_i.

*Stage-α step.* If the read refines at (λ, s) (a residual root of
multiplicity m ≥ 2 — so the leaf is not decided at this node), then by
LEMMA HE3-2(b) the recentered key Φ″ has a polygon whose portion above
λ has length exactly m, the corresponding block of D′m roots is
S_{λ, Z−s} (LEMMA HE3-4's computation, which did not use m = 1 —
it gives |S_{λ,Z−s}| = D′m), and this block's own labels are read at
Φ″. The blocks at slope λ with other residues, and at other slopes, are
untouched (their labels at Φ″ agree with those at Φ′ by LEMMA

label-class of a multiplicity-m system over the SAME stage ring, with
the same (e₁, f₁), so its pieces compose into the dictionary as claimed.
Termination: each refine strictly increases the floor λ and dv-heights
are bounded by the leaf's window (GENHN-3(a)), so the chain is finite —
and at a decided leaf it ends with all residuals separable.

*Stage-CS step.* If at some node the block's residual has a repeated
