1. **Critical — 𝒮ₙ^raw is not the catalogue enumerated in SP.6.**  
   SP.1 defines it as “all species tuples … whose integer fields satisfy (B1)–(B5).” Those inequalities do not impose the geometric compatibility used in the 53-letter enumeration—for example, the relation between anchor, side position, slot set, and flank spans. Consequently many formally budget-admissible tuples are absent from SP.6.

   There is also a direct contradiction inside the displayed data: (B5) asserts “anchors ≤ side width,” but R3 has \(a=2\) and \(w'=e\ell=1\). Thus the two R3 letters, and their Q3 copies, are excluded by the stated definition of 𝒮₃^raw. Hence \(|𝒮_3^{raw}|=53\) cannot follow from the definition as written.

2. **Critical — terminal verdicts are inconsistently typed, so per-read completeness and the 53-letter claim do not close.**  
   SP.1 says selection is either \((g,\mu)\) “or a terminal-verdict label,” making a verdict part of a full species tuple. SP.4 instead adds bare verdict labels to `Out(s)`, even though `Out(s)` was defined as a set of successor species, while SP.6 includes only five confirming/verdict species and says bare labels “close every remaining outcome.” A verdict is alternately:

   - a field of the current read’s letter,
   - a standalone successor,
   - or one of the special \(W=1\) letters.

   These are not the same object. Therefore SP-COMP’s verdict-read case and the claimed exhaustive 53-letter alphabet are not jointly well-typed.

3. **Critical — the entrance sub-catalogue is not proved finite.**  
   SP.4 argues that the alphabet and each successor menu are finite and therefore “with loops collapsed … \(\{\varepsilon\}\) is finite up to its … parameters.” This does not follow. A finite directed graph can have infinitely many collapsed walks through non-self cycles or arbitrarily long loop-free alternations. The note expressly disclaims any collapsed-length bound. It proves only finiteness at each fixed length, not finiteness of the entrance family required by the displayed claim.

4. **Critical — \(N^*(\text{letter})\) is not well-defined by the stated definition.**  
   It is defined as “the least \(N\) at which some … chain realizes it,” without specifying the prime. At a fixed prime, field-size-excluded letters never realize, so no least \(N\) exists—for example, the eleven excluded letters at \(p=2\). If “some prime” is intended, that quantifier is absent and p-independence of the resulting threshold is not derived. Accordingly the global “iff” prediction is not presently a well-formed consequence of the definition, independently of the correctness of the six numerical thresholds.
