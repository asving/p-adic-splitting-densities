I found two critical errors and five justification gaps. I treated §C as established and checked only whether §D4-R uses it with the correct scope and hypotheses.

1. **L1 does not prove canonical-choice totality. — JUSTIFICATION GAP**

> “*Sketch:* §B2-DEF's offset-P paragraph: the weights … clear the threshold when ν does … No node exists where the policy is partial. [Derivation-grade: to be re-derived fresh-context with the full boundary enumeration before D4-n3 seals.]”

The note advertises that it “Consumes ONLY” accepted §C, but §C merely names the offset-P lift as the canonical choice; it does not prove that this lift exists for every eligible residual class or in all the listed boundary cases. L1 instead relies on §B2-DEF and explicitly defers the required boundary enumeration.

Thus the claimed “for EVERY class,” including minimal ν, \(g=1\), and \(a>0\), is an unproved kernel lemma. Assuming L1, its later uses to make lifts deterministic are coherent.

2. **L5 asserts rather than derives the exhaustive next-outcome classification. — JUSTIFICATION GAP**

> “TOTALITY: the outcome case list is EXHAUSTIVE — the displayed obligation for the kernel derivation…”

and

> “such \(f\) lie in the deeper strata typed by higher valuation clauses — none orphaned”

and

> “[Derivation-grade with L1: the full polygon/side/outcome enumeration is the kernel's core burden.]”

The labels `(z)`, `(ep)`, and `(ns)` are not an exhaustion proof. In particular, “lie in the deeper strata” does not establish that every degeneracy determines one of the specified next-node data, nor is the absence of overlaps derived from the detailed polygon conventions.

This is exactly D4R.0’s central OPEN kernel, not a discharged lemma. Consequently L2’s total step map, D4R.0’s partition, and every later use of L5 are conditional on this gap.

3. **L5 does not cover the empty prefix needed for the root step in L6. — JUSTIFICATION GAP**

L5 begins:

> “Fix a realized prefix \(\eta\) with state cylinder \(\Sigma_k := \Psi_\eta(S(\eta,\top))\).”

But L3 defines a realized prefix as:

> “\(\eta=(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_k)\)”

so its stated scope begins after a root read. In the reverse inclusion of L6, however, the induction uses L5 at \(i=0\):

> “suppose \(\eta_{<i}\preceq H_{\rm can}^{\max}(f)\)… by L5's exclusivity the run's actual outcome at step \(i\) is the UNIQUE outcome…”

At \(i=0\), \(\eta_{<0}\) is the empty history. Although §C defines \(T(\varnothing,Z)\), L5 itself does not state its partition/exclusivity assertion for the empty-prefix/full-box state. Therefore the reverse inclusion in D4R-CYL is missing its base case.

Apart from this scope gap and the OPEN L5 kernel, the mandatory finite-or-infinite repair is logically sound: L6 does not invoke finite terminal completions, and infinite runs remain inside the right-hand prefix event.

4. **L11 does not establish that side-digit tuples determine all forgotten node data. — JUSTIFICATION GAP**

> “given the shape and the digit tuples … the node data \(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_{i-1}\) … are reconstructed”

and

> “read \(i\)'s node datum is its shape data … plus its digit tuple…”

The claimed injection is explicitly on the correct set,

> “\(\operatorname{Pref}(\widehat P)\hookrightarrow\prod_r\mathbb A_r^{d_r}\),”

but its proof assumes the key point. A §C node contains more history data than merely the retained shape fields and side digits—for example the residual factor/key data \(\psi_i\) used in `(HV)` and in later frames. The note gives no factor-selection or reconstruction lemma showing that equal side-digit tuples and equal degree/shape data force these data to agree, especially when one residual polynomial has multiple eligible factors of the same shape.

The `(NA)/(HV)` observation does not repair this:

> “they carve out a subset of the enumerated product and can never enlarge it.”

That statement is valid only after histories have been injected into the product. Predicates can remove encoded tuples, but they do not prevent multiple histories from sharing one tuple. L8, the pool bound, and hence D4R.2 remain conditional on this injection claim.

5. **\(N(\widehat P)\) is undefined when \(\operatorname{Pref}(\widehat P)\) is empty. — CRITICAL ERROR**

> “Define
> \[
> N(\widehat P):=\max_{\eta\in\operatorname{Pref}(\widehat P)}N(\eta,\top).
> \]
> Then \(N(\widehat P)\) is finite…”

L8 establishes at most that \(\operatorname{Pref}(\widehat P)\) is finite; it does not establish that it is nonempty. Realizability conditions may remove all value assignments, and the note nowhere restricts D4R.4 to shapes with \(C_{\widehat P}(p)>0\).

The maximum of an empty set is not defined under the displayed definition. Therefore D4R.4 is not a theorem for every fixed shape-prefix as stated. I stop treating its subsequent thresholded count formula—and gate clauses depending on that threshold—as established.

For a nonempty \(\operatorname{Pref}(\widehat P)\), the finite-maximum argument would otherwise be faithful: §C supplies a finite \(N(\eta,\top)\) for each realizable history, and L8 supplies finiteness of the index set, conditional on L11.

6. **The conditionality labels contradict the note’s claimed status. — JUSTIFICATION GAP**

> “D4R.1/2/4 are unconditional GIVEN §C (accepted) MODULO the kernel D4R.0, which is OPEN…”

and earlier:

> “ESTABLISHED HERE, exactly: … the partition D4R.0, the exact mass law D4R.1, the p-uniform inequality D4R.2 …”

“Unconditional … modulo [an] OPEN” kernel is conditional. Moreover, D4R.0 is listed as “ESTABLISHED HERE” even though L1 and L5 explicitly defer their derivation. The burden ledger correctly names totality, terminal exhaustiveness, and common-frame coherence, but it does not discharge the first two merely by assigning them lemma labels.

L4’s common-frame argument is otherwise adequate conditional on L1: §C C.0 explicitly says that a lift-carrying history determines every \(\Theta_i\) and \(\Psi_H\). The failure is the status claim, not the common-frame citation.

7. **The sealed-gate predictions are not derivable from the note text. — CRITICAL ERROR**

> “side data fixed, \(\mu=1\)”

and

> “SEAL BEFORE ANY CENSUS RUNS — each prediction derived from this note's displayed statements…”

followed by requests for:

> “\(C_{\widehat P^*}(p)\) at \(p=2\) and \(p=3\),”  
> “\(A(\widehat P^*)\),”  
> “TWO levels \(N\) each with \(N\ge N(\widehat P^*)\)”

No actual side data are displayed. Nor are the admissible digit tuples, the concrete `(NA)/(HV)` predicates, the fresh-digit ledger, \(A(\widehat P^*)\), \(W(\widehat P^*)\), \(N(\widehat P^*)\), or the two test levels specified. “Irreducible quadratic · linear” and “side data fixed” leave free choices that can alter the realized-prefix count and ledger.

Consequently a referee cannot derive or seal numerical predictions from this note alone. The formulas explain how numbers would be used after the missing instance data and enumeration are supplied; they do not determine those numbers. This defeats the stated prediction-before-census gate.

The §C citation at \(Z=\top\) is otherwise faithful. The empty equation system is admissible, has volume \(1\), and Theorem C(b) applies to every realizable lift-carrying prefix, not merely terminal histories. After normalization it gives exactly

\[
\mu(S(\eta,\top))
=\prod_i\operatorname{vol}(E_{\rm fresh}(\nu_i)).
\]

The note also handles `(NA)/(HV)` on its own side by restricting \(\operatorname{Pref}(\widehat P)\) to realizable histories, as §C requires. No illicit sum over terminating completions survives in D4R.1.
